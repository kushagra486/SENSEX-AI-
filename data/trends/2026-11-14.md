## What this means for us

Read this with two cautions. The scan covers YouTube search only. The short-videos section covers videos under four minutes, and only 5 of its 167 videos are under one minute, so true Shorts and Reels are still barely measured. The web sources below are industry reports and vendor blogs, not platform data.

### Short videos tell a different story from long ones

Added after a second scan limited to videos under four minutes.

- **Recurring mascot characters win.** "Sheru Bhai", a rich lion character, has at least four of the top short videos (316K, 108K, 107K and a 143K tutorial on how to copy the format). Viewers follow a character, not a plot.
- **Vegetable and animal love stories** repeat here too (Radish love story 103K, Potato and Radish 98K).
- **Horror does not carry over to short videos.** "AI horror story" has a median of 212 views in short videos, against 103,786 in long-form. The horror audience in this scan is watching 10 to 30 minute narrated stories.
- **Episode or part numbering appears in 22% of short videos**, more than double the long-form share (9%).
- **AI gameplay is weak in both scans** (median 208 in short videos).

What this means for the first script: "Chai at 3:07" is a horror series in one-minute episodes, a combination this scan gives no evidence for. Its series structure and recurring characters match what works; its genre is a bet. A safer first test by this data is a recurring comic character in two to three minute episodes.

### Storylines pulling the most views

1. **Local horror tied to a real place or festival.** "Diwali wali Chudail" (4.5M), "Haunted Ladakh Trip / Manali Leh Highway Ghost" (811K), "Dadi's ghost story that came true" (725K). "Horror" is the second most common title word (20 videos).
2. **Rags-to-riches and hidden identity.** Goddess Lakshmi visits a poor potter (1.1M), the rejected girl who is secretly the Dragon King's daughter (668K), the beggar whose real identity shocks everyone (535K), the secret billionaire divorce (330K).
3. **Family cruelty and moral payback.** Father throws out his 8-year-old daughter (486K), mother-in-law against pregnant daughter-in-law (491K), the jealous aunt (576K). Mostly Hindi and Urdu, 20 to 30 minutes.
4. **Talking vegetable and animal 3D comedy.** Potato and Radish's jungle romance (2.0M), the poor potato who becomes a dairy owner (593K), Sheru the lion stranded on an island (444K).
5. **"World after" sci-fi short films.** "The World After World War III" (3.0M). One video, so an anecdote, but the biggest English-language hit in the scan.

### Formats

- On long-form YouTube, 10 to 30 minute videos have the highest median views (201K across 70 videos). These are narrated stories with simple repeated visuals, which is how channels afford the length.
- India's micro-drama market (vertical episodes of one to two minutes) is growing fast, and 89% of viewers find these shows through social feeds, not apps ([Meta-Ormax report](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/social-feeds-are-powering-indias-micro-drama-boom-meta-ormax-report/)). 47% of viewers call AI-made micro-dramas unique and creative; 38% prefer real actors ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/are-micro-dramas-the-next-big-thing-in-india-47-find-ai-generated-content-creative-says-report-521234-2026-03-18)).
- With free video credits, a 20-minute video is out of reach. A vertical series of 45 to 60 second episodes fits both the credits and the micro-drama trend.

### Scenes and gameplay

- "AI generated gameplay" was the weakest search by far (median 13,651 views). Its top result is ordinary GTA 6 commentary, not AI video. The scan gives no evidence that AI-made gameplay scenes are a strong niche right now.
- Recurring settings in the hits: villages and courtyards, night highways, festival nights, a poor home visited by someone powerful.

### Hooks and titles

- 36% of top titles contain a number and 32% an ALL-CAPS word.
- Titles ending in an ellipsis cliffhanger ("...then what happened") have a higher median (271K, 13 videos) than the scan overall.
- Hidden-identity and revenge words have the highest medians (298K and 330K) but appear in only 5 and 3 videos, so treat that as a hint.
- Emoji and the language name ("Hindi", "Urdu", "Tamil") in the title are standard in the regional hits.

### Reach

- Language is the biggest lever visible in the data: most of the top "AI story" results are Hindi, Urdu, Bengali or Tamil. "Hindi" appears in 15 titles.
- Seasonal hooks work: five titles in the top list are Diwali-themed.
- Series labelling ("Part 1", "Part 403") appears in 9% of videos, including the single biggest one (15.1M).
- Channels with several hits this week (Little Dream Village, Vaibhu ai, Scary Owl) post in one narrow theme.

### Gaps

- Horror and hidden-identity stories are almost all long narrated videos. Few channels in the scan tell them as short vertical episodes with a cliffhanger.
- English-language Indian-set stories are rare among the hits; that is either a gap or a sign the audience wants its own language. Testing one series in Hindi would answer it.
